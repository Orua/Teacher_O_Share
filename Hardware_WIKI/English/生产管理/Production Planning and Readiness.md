# Production Planning and Readiness

Before starting a production run of buckles, confirm more than the availability of metal. The drawing, colour sample, mould, springs, gauges and inspection method must all be ready for use. This page explains how to confirm production readiness and reduce avoidable stoppages or rework.

## Start with the approved basis

Check that the current drawing, 3D data, physical sample, material, finish, function, packing and inspection method are consistent. If the drawing and sample differ, record which one takes precedence before releasing the work. Any change that affects tooling, assembly, appearance or inspection requires an impact assessment for material already produced.

## “Ready” means usable

Confirm each item below, not merely that it exists:

- correct material grade, condition, batch and usable quantity;
- mould, fixture and gauge at the correct revision and in working condition;
- calibrated measuring tools and suitable equipment;
- body, springs, pins, magnets, screws, chains or bought-in parts in the right quantity and status;
- approved colour sample, pretreatment, rack or fixture and coverage requirement;
- bags, separators, labels, boxes and other packing protection;
- trained staff and a clearly defined next operation.

The body and components must be counted separately. If a product needs two components per set, the number of sets that can be assembled is limited by the smallest usable component quantity divided by the quantity required per set.

## Decide what quantity to release

The order quantity is the committed accepted quantity. The manufacturing release quantity may include approved first pieces, test samples, normal process loss and a reasonable contingency. The manufacturing release quantity should not be used to conceal an unstable process. Later additions should be identified as a normal quantity top-up, rework replacement, remanufacture or returned-goods replacement and linked to the original order.

## Set production priorities

Give higher priority to work with an approaching due date when all critical inputs are ready. Prepare early when external finishing has a long lead time or capacity will soon become available at a bottleneck. Do not release work when a critical material, component, revision, colour standard or inspection rule is missing. A conditional start may cover only an approved trial or unaffected operation; record the maximum quantity and stop condition.

Keep the original plan, revised plan and actual timing separate. This shows whether a delay resulted from an unrealistic plan, missing input, equipment problem, external processing or quality issue.

## Before the first piece

Confirm the revision, quantity, material and components; check the tools, gauges and equipment; confirm the process route, external stages, first-piece and final checks; prepare the packing and labels; and record any open risks and assigned responsibilities. Release the work to the shop floor only after these checks are complete.
